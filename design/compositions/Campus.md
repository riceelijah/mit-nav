# MIT campus navigation

The map covers the MIT campus. Every building is a place, and buildings are joined by outdoor walks along OpenStreetMap's footpaths, sidewalks and crossings; a walk that uses steps, or a path marked as not for wheelchairs, is not step-free, unless the steps are mapped with a wheelchair ramp alongside or as usable by wheelchairs. These walks (between path points, from a building to the paths, and the straight walks between buildings far from any path) are outdoor; every connection inside or into a building is not. A building's place is its main entrance; each of its other mapped entrances is a place of its own at the door, only passed through, joined to the path point nearest it outside and to the ground-floor corridor nearest it inside, so a route enters by the entrance it reaches rather than crossing the building to the main one; a building joins the paths at each entrance, or at the path point nearest it when it has none; a building with no path within 80 metres is joined by straight walks to its nearest buildings instead, so it stays reachable. Path points are only passed through and are never named in directions; a sidewalk without a name of its own takes the name of the street it runs beside (the nearest named street within 25 metres), and a crossing is called a crosswalk. The page shows consecutive outdoor walks along the same street as one step that adds up their metres, starting a new step where the name changes, with the turn there (left, right, slight left or right, or straight on, from the change in heading); unnamed stretches join the step before them. The short walk from the paths to a building's entrance ends the outdoor step before it, so arriving at the entrance is part of that step. Inside the buildings whose room lists have been converted, each floor is a network of its corridors, lobbies and bridges, placed where MIT's floor plans put them and joined to one another by walking distance: each corridor to its three nearest corridors within 40 metres, then, while the floor is in separate pieces, the closest pair of corridors joining two pieces. Each corridor is a place that is only passed through, named by its room number and use ("32-100LA Lobby"); room numbers are unique and corridors are never also listed as rooms, so these names never collide with a room's. MIT's floor plans give positions by room label, and those labels were read off the drawings by machine, so some are misread ("1001A" for 100LA, "1448" for 144B); a label that matches no room on its floor is read again treating L, I and 1, O, D, Q and 0, S and 5, B and 8, Z and 2, G and 6, and T and 7 as the same, and placed only when exactly one unplaced room on that floor reads the same way. Every room with a known position attaches to the corridor nearest it, and also to the next nearest when that one is within 25 metres, so a large room such as a lecture hall can be reached from either side; a room without one attaches to the corridor nearest the middle of that floor's corridors and takes that corridor's position. Each elevator stop and stairway landing is a place that is only passed through, at its position on its floor ("32-100E1 Elevator", "3-100SA Stairway"), reached by a walk from the corridor nearest it (or the floor's middle corridor when it has no position, whose position it then takes); each ride or flight is a connection between two such places, so a route changes floor at the stairs or elevator itself, and the page shows the walk to it and the ride as one step ("Walk about 10 m to stairway SA and take it up to floor 2"), flagged by the ride. A floor whose corridors have no known positions is a single corridor instead, with its rooms opening off it.

A connection's distance is a cost, not a length: walking inside costs 0.2 plus the metres walked over 50, walking outside along a path the metres over 50, and the walk between a building's door and the paths, or a straight walk between two buildings far from any path, 1 plus the metres over 50, so a path is not penalised for its bends, and an elevator ride 3.5 plus half a unit per floor plus the walks to and from it. The route is the cheapest trip under those costs, so it takes the elevator that makes the whole trip shortest, counting the walk to each elevator, rather than simply the one nearest the destination. Each connection's directions carry the metres themselves ("Walk about 30 m to elevator E3 and take it up to floor 4"), and when the page shows several corridor walks in a row as one step, it adds up the metres written in them.

The ground floor is floor 1, or the lowest floor above it when there is no floor 1, or the lowest floor. A building is entered from the corridor nearest each of its OpenStreetMap entrances within 15 metres of its outline; with no such entrance, or when the ground floor is a single corridor, it is entered at the corridor nearest the building's centre, or at that single corridor. Two buildings whose outlines touch are joined indoors on a floor when each has a corridor on that floor within 35 metres of the other's outline, joining each building's corridor nearest the other building's outline; the floor plans mark a corridor at its middle, so corridors that meet at a shared wall are usually 20 to 50 metres apart as marked. Corridors of any two buildings within 15 metres of each other on a floor, such as the two ends of a bridge, are joined as well. Where both buildings' floors have traced outlines (see below) and their traced corridors, lobbies, bridges or unlabelled open floor come within 15 metres of each other, such a join is made instead between each building's traced corridor nearest the point where those spaces come closest, through that point; whether two buildings join on a floor is still decided as above, since a wall without a door looks the same up close. When either building's floor has no positions, they are joined on that floor if their outlines touch, from each building's corridor nearest the other's centre (or its single corridor).

Simmons Hall is generated from its room list like every other building. The campus has thousands of places and connections, so the app never sends the whole map to the browser. A visitor finds a place by typing part of its name: the [place search](former:Campus.Browsing.PlaceSearch) answers at most 40 matching places that can be a start or a destination, with entrances first (every building is an entrance), and the [search request](reaction:Campus.Browsing.SearchPlaces) carries the typed text and answers with those places; when nothing matches the answer is an empty list, not a refusal. Anyone can also see the problems reported so far: the [problem list](former:Campus.Browsing.ProblemList) lists open reports, each with the reporter's username from `Registering._username` (a report whose reporter has no username, such as one made before accounts existed, is still listed, with no name, so an open problem is never hidden), the names of its connection's two places and its directions, and the [blocked list](former:Campus.Browsing.BlockedList) lists every blocked connection the same way, including any blocked without a report, so it can still be marked clear. The [overview request](reaction:Campus.Browsing.ShowOverview) takes no input and answers with both lists. Generated place names begin with the building ("Building 32, floor 1", "32-123 Lecture Hall"), so typing a building or room number finds them.

The page can also show floor plans. With the campus map the converter writes one plan file per converted building, which the frontend serves: the building's outline and, for each floor (named as MIT names it, such as "1" or "G4", with the same floor number its places carry, so "G4" is floor 4, and "00", the level below "0", is floor -1), every room MIT's inventory places there with a position (offices, labs, closets, shafts, stairs, elevators, corridors, everything), with its room number, use, position and floor area. Service rooms (shaft spaces and electric, telecom, utility and custodial closets) stay in the plan files but are never drawn, labelled or traced: they are mostly small rooms inside corridor walls, and drawing them would cover the corridor. These rooms are drawn, not routed: only the places described above are in the map, so a closet never appears in search or in a route. The page draws only what it knows the shape of: the building's outline; its corridors, lobbies, bridges, private circulation and unlabelled open floor (described below) where they were traced from MIT's drawings, each drawn as straight bars rather than its traced outline (the converter cuts each such piece, turned to the building's longest edge, on a half-metre grid into the largest rectangles it holds, one after another, until nine tenths of it is covered or the next rectangle is under 4 square metres, so an L-shaped corridor is two bars and doorway notches, tracing jitter and odd slivers are left out), tinted, and traced lounges more lightly, in their traced shape; elevators and stairs as small squares of their floor area at their positions, since those are fixed fittings of known size; and every other room as its room number at its position, with no shape, because the inventory knows a room's position and size but not its walls, and a guessed wall looks like a wrong one. The destination is drawn in its traced shape when it has one, and otherwise ringed at its position; every plan is drawn beneath the route line. The page says the plan is a schematic of where things are, not MIT's drawing of the walls. The square of a room's floor area around its position, turned to line up with the outline's longest edge (3 metres square for a room with no area), is what the converter uses as the room's shape for route lines when the room is not traced; the page does not draw it. Where MIT's own drawing of a floor has been downloaded and fitted to the map, the page can show that instead: a separate local step reads the room labels on the drawing, fits the drawing to the map by the positions of the labels it shares with the floor plans (turning, scaling and moving it, never stretching it), and keeps the fit only when at least 6 labels match and half of them land within 2 metres of their positions; the frontend serves each fitted drawing with its fit, and the floor switcher then offers "Schematic" and "MIT drawing" for that floor, remembering the choice. These drawings stay on the machine that made them, out of the repository and the deployed app, because MIT Facilities requires written permission to copy or display them; without them the page shows the schematic only and offers no choice. The same local step also traces room outlines from each fitted drawing, replacing that floor's earlier outlines each time it runs. The rooms are those the floor's plan file places on that floor, at the positions described above (misread labels already recovered); the drawing's own labels only fit the drawing and never name a room. It finds the open floor inside the drawing's walls and within the building's outline (plus a metre's margin, since the outline and the drawing can disagree slightly); an area that reaches the margin's outer edge (a corridor open at the building's end, where the next building carries it on, runs out into the margin around the outside) is cut back to the outline plus 0.3 metres before anything below, and each room in it keeps only the piece of its share around its position; it closes doorways of up to about 0.9 metres without thickening walls or door swings, and ignores text and other marks under 0.9 metres across. Each room's space is the open area around its position. When several rooms share one open area, each room that is not a corridor, lobby or bridge grows from its position through that area, never across a drawn line, until it reaches its inventory area (3 m² at least), and the corridors, lobbies and bridges among them take the rest, each the part nearest its position; with none among them, the rest is left as unlabelled open floor. A room whose own open area is under half its inventory area (3 m² or more), such as a seated hall the drawing's lines split into rows, also takes in the unlabelled open areas just across a line under 0.1 metres thick from it, nearest its position first, while it stays under 1.3 times its area, until it has 0.85 of it; when it cannot reach half that way (seating drawn so densely that closing doorways filled it), it takes instead the largest piece of drawn ink beside its area that is more than a metre thick throughout (no wall is), when that brings it to between half and 1.3 times its area; otherwise it keeps its own area only. For an area several rooms' positions lie in, this applies to the largest of them, and the area is then split among them as described above. Each traced space is then grown back into the doorways that were closed, never across a drawn line, so spaces on either side of a door meet in the doorway. Open floor that no room's position lies in is kept as unlabelled open floor too: these are mostly corridor stretches no label names. Unlabelled open floor of either kind is kept only when it is at least 4 m², does not reach the margin's outer edge (the strip outside the building's walls), is not taken in by a hall as above, and is not a box the drawing crosses out (marking an opening to the floor below, or a shaft): neither a three-cornered quarter of one (corners clipped by under 0.6 metres still count), nor one whose diagonals are both drawn, however faintly. For drawing, each corridor, lobby, bridge and piece of unlabelled open floor is also kept as it was before being grown into its doorways (bounded by its walls and the closed doorways, so without the notches doorways leave), cut to the outline the same way; the page draws those, and route lines use the grown outlines. Finally every traced space from the footprint-bounded tracing and every piece of unlabelled open floor is cut to the building's outline itself, without the margin, so no such space, and no route line drawn through one, reaches outdoors through a doorway too wide to close, such as a double entrance door; the area checks and the 4 m² minimum apply before this cut, and a space the cut leaves empty is dropped. Recovered outlines (below) are not cut, since they lie beyond the outline by nature, and the route line only ever starts or ends in one. A room the footprint keeps from tracing (part of a building that reaches beyond its outline, such as a basement under a plaza) keeps the outline the same tracing finds without the footprint, when that one passes the checks below, overlaps no outline kept from the bounded tracing beyond the wall they share, and is not a corridor, lobby or bridge (so the drawn route line is never led outdoors through one); the plan file marks such recovered outlines, and the route line passes through one only when it is the walk's own start or end; a cut-off room that fails these stays untraced and is drawn approximately, as described above. A traced outline is kept only when its area is within a factor of two of the inventory's; a room whose inventory area is under 3 m², or missing, is not checked. For a shared open area it is also kept when the whole area is within a factor of two of the sharers' combined known area (not checked when that is under 3 m²). Elevators and stairways are never traced; a service room is never drawn, but an open area its position lies in alone stays its own (so a closet is never counted as open floor), while an area it shares with other rooms goes to them as if it were not there (most closets' positions lie in the corridor outside them); seated halls are kept like any other room when they pass the check, which their seating rows usually prevent. Only these outlines are kept, never the drawing's walls, text or other lines; the converter adds them, and each floor's unlabelled open floor, to the plan files, so they are committed with them, first dropping every corner within 0.4 metres of the line between its neighbours, so tracing jitter and small notches do not show. On a machine without a building's local outlines, the converter keeps the outlines that building's current plan file already has, for rooms still on the same floor, with their recovered marks, and each floor's unlabelled open floor, so regenerating elsewhere never drops them. The schematic draws a room with its traced outline when it has one, and as described above otherwise. The page tells which building a route step is in from the building number its generated name begins with ("32-"). At zoom 18 and closer it shows the plan of each building on the route whose outline is in view, and hides them when zoomed out; to open one by hand, the route panel lists the route's buildings, and choosing one zooms the map to it. The panel lists no floors: while a building's plan shows, a floor switcher on the map lists the floors in that building's plan file, marks those whose floor number a route step in that building carries, and switches the plan; the plan opens on the floor of the step the person is on when that step is in the building, otherwise on the first floor the route uses there, and follows the current step to other floors. Wherever the route changes floor inside a building, the line carries a badge saying up or down and the floor it reaches, one for each run of stairs or rides, with that run's directions on hover; choosing the badge zooms in there and shows that floor's plan. While a building's plan shows, the route line inside that building draws only the walks on the floor shown (a walk to stairs or an elevator is on the floor it leaves), so it never runs across a floor the route is not on there; zoomed out, with no plans showing, the whole line is drawn.

```endpoints
Campus.Browsing.ShowOverview at /map/overview
Campus.Browsing.SearchPlaces at /map/search
```

A visitor picks where they are and where they are going, says whether they need a route without stairs and whether they would rather stay indoors (for the winter), and gives the time of day, because a passage can be closed at some hours of the day; the generated campus map has no closing hours yet, so today every hour gives the same route. The [route](former:Campus.Routing.Route) is computed from the connections that are neither blocked nor closed at that hour, so [asking for a route](reaction:Campus.Routing.FindRoute) never sends anyone through a passage someone has flagged. The request carries the two places, the step-free flag, the indoors flag, and the hour, and answers with the numbered steps, each carrying the connection it uses, which is where a visitor starts when flagging a passage; when either place is unknown or no route exists, the answer is an empty list rather than a refusal. The places must be text, both flags true or false, and the hour a number from 0 to 24; anything else is refused with INVALID_REQUEST. Such checks, here and in every endpoint below, are made by the endpoint's input validator on the request alone, before any concept is consulted.

```endpoints
Campus.Routing.FindRoute at /route/find
```

A visitor can also ask for the nearest place of a kind, such as a restroom, from where they are: the [nearest-of-a-kind request](reaction:Campus.Routing.FindClosest) carries the start, the text that names the kind (the page offers Restroom, Food, Study space and Lounge, sending "Lavatory", "Food Facility", "Study" and "Lounge", the room-use words in generated names), both flags and the hour, and answers, from the [closest former](former:Campus.Routing.Closest), up to five such places ordered by the cost of reaching them under the same rules as a route. Choosing one makes it the destination. The start, flags and hour are required and checked as for a route, and the text must be text; anything else is refused with INVALID_REQUEST. An unknown start, or nothing matching, answers an empty list.

```endpoints
Campus.Routing.FindClosest at /route/closest
```

Every place known to the campus map has a position: a building's is its main entrance (the mapped entrance marked main, else the entrance nearest its centre, else the centre of its outline); corridors and rooms placed from the floor plans have their own; a room on such a floor without one takes the position of the corridor it attaches to; and a floor that is a single corridor, with its rooms, shares its building's. A place added with latitude and longitude both 0 has none, and the page leaves such places off the map and out of every distance check. Each route step carries the kind and position of the place it arrives at, and each search result carries a position, so the page draws the route as a line through those positions, with the start and end marked. Indoors, a straight line between two places can cross walls, so the converter gives every indoor connection on a traced floor the path a person walks along it, found once over the floor's traced spaces, and each route step carries its connection's path in the direction walked; the page draws the route as each step's path ending at its place, so the line and the route come from one model and the page searches nothing. The path is the cheapest path on a half-metre grid, where a step through a traced corridor, lobby, bridge or lounge (open public space such as Stata's Student Street), the floor's unlabelled open floor, or either of the connection's two places counts once, and a step through any other traced room twenty times (so the line goes through a room, by its doors, only where the way round by corridor is more than twenty times as long as the way through it), with corners kept only where a straight line would leave that area or cross a room the path itself did not go through. The path is searched over the whole floor: everything inside the building's outline is open floor except the rooms, each by its traced outline or, without one, the square of its floor area described above, widened by half a metre so the cracks between schematic shapes close; corridors, lobbies, bridges, private circulation and traced open floor count as open floor, as the bars the page draws of them together with their traced outlines (the slivers the bars leave out are mostly doorways), so the line runs where a corridor is shown and still gets through its doors, and traced lounges in their shape, even where a schematic shape covers them; a step onto open floor beside a wall or a room costs a little more, so the line keeps to the middle of a corridor. Before paths are found, a corridor marked beside its traced floor is moved onto the open floor nearest it, within 6 metres, so the line runs along the corridor rather than from label to label. A place with a traced outline is joined through its door, at the open floor within 4 metres of it; a place with no outline of its own, such as a seated lecture hall, is met at the open floor nearest it within 15 metres that the walk reaches, and the path then runs straight from there to its position. A connection between two buildings on one floor is one walk over both floors together, with a strip 3 metres wide between the two places counting as open floor so the gap between the outlines (a door, bridge or passage) can be crossed; rooms in either building still count as rooms. A building whose floor has no plan contributes nothing but its place, which the strip reaches. A recovered outline (see the floor plans above) counts only when it is one of the two places, never as a room passed through on the way. The connection's distance and the metres in its directions are then that path's length, so the route prefers the way that is shorter on foot and the step text matches the line. A connection whose two ends the traced floor does not join (an untraced stretch, or a doorway no drawing shows) keeps its straight line and its label-to-label metres; the converter reports how many such connections there are. Connections that are not indoors, or not on a traced floor, have no path and are drawn straight between their places. The off-course check below measures against the straight lines between the route's places, so rerouting is unchanged.

Location stays off unless the person turns it on, or, when they are signed in, their saved preference to use their location (see [saved things](Saved.md)) turns it on when the page loads; turning it off stops the page watching at once. While it is on, the page follows progress itself. Only a step that arrives at an entrance (a building, reached by walking outside) is marked done automatically, when a reading is within 40 metres of that building's position, and every step before it is marked done too; inside a building a phone cannot tell where it is, so the person marks indoor steps done by hand, and consecutive walks along corridors are shown as one step. The first step not yet done is highlighted.

The page measures "off course" as the ground distance from a reading to the route as straight lines between its places (indoor paths drawn along corridors are not used). If two readings in a row are more than 75 metres from it, the page asks for the [nearest building](reaction:Campus.Routing.NearestPlace) and finds a new route from there to the same destination, with the same step-free choice and the current time. It then waits for two fresh readings before it can reroute again, since a reading can be off by tens of metres between buildings. When there is no nearest building or no new route, the page says so and keeps the old route. The request carries a latitude and longitude, which must be numbers (anything else is refused with INVALID_REQUEST), and answers with the closest entrance from the [nearest-place former](former:Campus.Routing.Nearest), or nothing. A person's position is never stored; it is sent only to find the nearest building.

```endpoints
Campus.Routing.NearestPlace at /route/nearest
```

Searching and routing need no account, but flagging and clearing do, so every report belongs to a person (see [accounts](Accounts.md)). A signed-in person can [flag a connection as blocked](reaction:Campus.Problems.BlockLink) with a reason. The endpoint passes the session to `Sessioning.use`, which returns the person or refuses with NOT_SIGNED_IN, and the person, never a request input, becomes the report's reporter. The flag takes effect in the connection itself, and the reporter and reason are kept as a report, so the two are recorded in one request. The request carries the session, the connection, and the reason, and answers with the report. The connection is blocked first, and the report is recorded only once the block has succeeded, so a refused block records nothing: an unknown connection is refused with NO_SUCH_LINK, and one that is already blocked with ALREADY_BLOCKED. Because every ride of one elevator shares a service, flagging any ride takes the whole elevator out, and flagging another ride of an elevator that is already out is refused with ALREADY_BLOCKED without a second report. If recording the report fails after the block, the connection stays blocked without a report; anyone can mark it clear and flag it again.

```endpoints
Campus.Problems.BlockLink at /problems/block
Campus.Problems.UnblockLink at /problems/unblock
```

Any signed-in person can [mark a connection as clear again](reaction:Campus.Problems.UnblockLink); the endpoint starts with `Sessioning.use` the same way. Clearing is not limited to the reporter, because a passage would otherwise stay blocked whenever its reporter is gone. Because the report belongs to a different concept than the connection, [clearing a connection resolves its open reports](reaction:Campus.Problems.UnblockResolvesReports) as a reaction, rather than the connection concept knowing about reports. The request carries the session and the connection and answers with the connection; an unknown connection is refused with NO_SUCH_LINK, and one that is not blocked with NOT_BLOCKED. Clearing one ride of an elevator clears every ride that shares its service, so [clearing also resolves the reports on those rides](reaction:Campus.Problems.ClearingResolvesSharedReports); otherwise a report against another ride would stay open after the elevator is back.

The map itself is not editable over the API: there are no endpoints for adding places or connections, so a visitor cannot change it. A seed script, run by whoever deploys the app, fills it. The script is an operator tool outside the application, like a database migration, and runs only while the app is stopped, so it is the only writer while it runs and enforces the rules below itself: it writes the campus map generated from MIT's room inventory, MapIT's room positions, and OpenStreetMap's buildings, entrances and paths straight into Wayfinding's state by the rules below, and running it again with the same map changes nothing, so a run that stops partway is repaired by running it again. Running it after the map is regenerated updates the map in place rather than starting over. A place whose name is unchanged keeps its identity, with its kind, floor and position updated, so favorites, saved trips and history that refer to it keep working. A connection keeps its identity when it joins the same two places in the same direction with the same directions apart from their metres ("Walk about 30 m to stairway SA" and "Walk about 32 m to stairway SA" are the same connection), with its other details updated and whether it is blocked kept; a new connection that shares a service with a blocked one starts blocked, since that elevator is still out. Places and connections no longer in the map are removed, and favorites and saved trips that refer to a removed place are removed with it (history entries are kept, so totals still count them). Every run, after removing, resolves every open report whose connection is no longer in the map, so a run that stopped partway leaves no report open on a passage that is gone once it is run again. Accounts, sessions, preferences, history and every other report are left alone.
